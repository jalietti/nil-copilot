## Phase 1: Look Inward

### What is the problem? (Symptoms)
Both, roughly equal weight:
- **Customer pain point** — student-athletes want NIL income but can't effectively find or negotiate deals themselves (packed schedules, no accessible agent).
- **Observed market pattern** — a glut of athletes on NIL marketplaces (Mogul, Influencer, etc.) chasing very few, mostly small/generic brands. Oversupply of athletes, undersupply of real brand fit.

### Why haven't we solved it?
- **Systemic inequity** — attention and deal flow concentrate on high-profile sports; athletes in sports like water polo, or with small followings, get left out despite having real value to offer.
- **Lack of resources (athlete side)** — full-time student + full-time athlete leaves no time for a job, let alone real brand outreach and negotiation.

### How are we part of the problem? (Builder's assumptions & biases)
- **Optimizing for own situation** — built from Jason's own sport, interests (outdoor/niche gear), and team; may not generalize to revenue-sport athletes or very different brand landscapes.
- **Solution-first thinking** — jumped to "build an outreach/negotiation copilot" before deep validation beyond his own team.

**Which of these might be redesigned, reframed, or removed?** Validate the brand-matching and negotiation-preference logic against athletes outside water polo before assuming it generalizes. Treat "own team" validation as a starting sample, not full coverage.

## Phase 2: Look Outward

### Who experiences the problem?
**Who:** Student-athletes broadly. Hits hardest for those without large scholarships who need money for tuition/rent and can't hold an outside job — and separately, athletes whose real interests (e.g., hiking/backpacking gear) don't match what's on NIL marketplaces at all.

**When/where:** Chronic and structural, not tied to a specific season or event — the mismatch between crowded marketplaces and narrow brand participation vs. the broader/niche interests of most athletes.

**Consequences:** Real NIL money goes unclaimed by exactly the people who need it most. Athletes don't benefit from NIL the way it's designed to help them.

### Who else has this problem? Who doesn't?
**Who else:** Freelance creators and anyone building a following around a genuine passion face the same squeeze — niche interest, no agent, no leverage to reach the right brand partners.

**Who doesn't have it:** Athletes with large existing followings who are willing to partner with whatever brand notices them, regardless of fit. Brands find them easily; they're already good at producing content; no matching problem to solve.

### Who's been left out? Who benefits?
**Left out:**
- International student-athletes — concrete case: a Dutch teammate, financially struggling, has never considered NIL a real option because the marketplaces are confusing and he doesn't know how to self-market. **Important:** international athletes may also face real visa/immigration restrictions on accepting NIL income — a separate, higher-stakes issue from the state agent-registration question, and a hard requirement (not a nice-to-have) that onboarding ask citizenship/visa status early and give real guidance rather than silence.
- Athletes with small or no following yet, and anyone who doesn't see themselves as "marketable."

**Who benefits when the problem exists (status quo):** NIL marketplaces profit from listing volume regardless of whether athletes actually land good deals. Athletes already winning under the current system face less competition for brand attention.

**Who benefits when it's solved:** The athlete/creator, but also the niche brands who currently can't find good matches either — a two-sided underserved market, not just one-sided.

## Phase 3: Reframe

### Stated another way, the problem is:
Student-athletes struggle to land brand deals that actually fit who they are, because the only channel realistically available to them rewards existing follower count and generic content over authentic fit, and because traditional agents, while legally permitted, are too expensive and uninterested in small or niche deals for non-revenue-sport or low-follower athletes. This leads to real NIL money going unclaimed — especially by those without big scholarships, international students, and athletes whose interests don't match what marketplaces offer — while the marketplaces profit off listing volume regardless of whether anyone actually gets a good deal.

### How Might We...
**How might we** help student-athletes land NIL deals that genuinely fit who they are — without needing an inaccessible traditional agent or settling for whatever a crowded marketplace surfaces — **as we aim to** get real NIL money moving for every student-athlete, particularly the ones the current system overlooks most: small-following, non-revenue-sport, international, financially strapped?

## Decisions locked during this session
- **Interaction model:** copilot, not agent. Drafts outreach and negotiation; the athlete is always the sender and final approver. Avoids most state athlete-agent registration exposure.
- **V1 scope:** all student-athletes, not narrowed to the underserved segment — built with them in mind, not built exclusively for them.
- **Monetization:** leaning subscription over a deal-contingent cut (contingent fees are a classic agent-law trigger regardless of who signs); real legal review needed before launch either way. Free-for-friends first, monetization model decided later.
- **Instagram integration:** cut from v1.
- **Social/audience-growth feature:** cut from v1, logged as a possible v2 direction — different product (content strategy) from the negotiation copilot.
- **International athlete handling:** required in v1 — citizenship/visa question early in onboarding, with real guidance attached, not silence.

## Open questions
- Brand-sourcing mechanism: curated-by-category vs. dynamic discovery — unresolved.
- "Good deal" logic: resolved for v1 — athlete-stated preference per brand tier (follower count × product value), no external market comp data needed.
