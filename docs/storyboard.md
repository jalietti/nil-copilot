# Storyboard: NIL Copilot (name TBD)

**Date:** 2026-08-04
**Main character:** Alex Rivera, 20, junior, Division II track & field, partial scholarship (tuition only). Genuinely into sustainable/outdoor brands. A few hundred Instagram followers — not enough to make brands come looking.
**Visual style:** Simple wireframe-style sketches, minimal and monochrome.

---

## Frame 1: Introducing the Main Character

**Narrative:** Alex Rivera, 20, junior at a Division II school, runs track on a partial scholarship that covers tuition but not rent or food. Between classes, practice, and travel meets, there's no time for an outside job.

**Visual:** Alex at a cramped dorm desk, cleats by the door, laptop open next to a stack of textbooks.
**Mood:** Busy, stretched thin, not desperate yet.

---

## Frame 2: The Problem Emerges

**Narrative:** Alex has heard NIL could help and checks the marketplace platform the team uses. Same handful of brands every time — protein powder, energy drinks, a mobile game. Nothing that fits. With only a few hundred followers, none of those brands are exactly competing for Alex's attention either.

**Visual:** Laptop screen showing a grid of generic brand logos, Alex scrolling with a flat expression.
**Mood:** Mildly discouraged, going through the motions.

---

## Frame 3: The "Oh Crap" Moment

**Narrative:** Rent's due in five days and Alex is $340 short after an unreimbursed team travel fee ate into what should've covered it. Scrolling the platform again out of desperation, Alex sees a teammate with a similar follower count just landed a $200 gift card from a brand they don't even care about — just better timing. Alex feels behind, and genuinely out of options before the deadline. (The $340 gets handled the ordinary way — a shift picked up, a roommate covers a few days — this moment is what pushes Alex to try something new, not something the copilot magically resolves.)

**Visual:** Phone screen showing a bank balance next to a rent due-date reminder; a group chat message from a teammate showing off a brand gift card.
**Mood:** Anxious, a little envious, out of patience with the status quo.

---

## Frame 4: The Solution Appears

**Narrative:** A teammate mentions in the group chat that they've been using a different tool — one that actually got them a deal with a brand they liked, not just whatever showed up on the marketplace. Alex, skeptical but out of better ideas, decides to give it a shot.

**Visual:** Group chat bubble: "yo this thing actually got me a deal w/ [brand] — you should try it." Alex tapping a link.
**Mood:** Skeptical curiosity.

---

## Frame 5: The "Aha" Moment

**Narrative:** Alex answers a short interview about interests and what actually matters in a partnership — mentions caring specifically about sustainable outdoor gear. The copilot drafts an outreach email to a small gear brand Alex has genuinely used and likes, one Alex wouldn't have thought to cold-email. Alex tweaks a line and sends it themselves. Three days later, a reply: the brand's interested. The copilot drafts a counter reflecting what Alex said mattered — some product, plus a modest payment given the smaller following. Alex reviews it, approves it, sends it. The payoff isn't "I'm rich now" — it's "something I said mattered, and it actually did something."

**Visual:** Split screen — interview chat bubble ("what brands do you actually care about?") on one side, drafted email with Alex's one edited line on the other; then a phone notification: "new reply from [brand]."
**Mood:** Focused, then quietly relieved and a little proud.

---

## Frame 6: Life After the Solution

**Narrative:** Two months later, Alex has three small partnerships running — a discount plus modest payment from that outdoor brand, product from a second, a third in negotiation. None individually life-changing, but they add up, and they're all things Alex would post about anyway. Next time a surprise cost hits, Alex isn't refreshing a marketplace at 11pm hoping something lands — there's already a pipeline moving, and asking the copilot to reach out to one more brand takes ten minutes, not a scramble.

**Visual:** Alex's phone showing three ongoing brand threads in a simple dashboard view; Alex at the same desk, relaxed posture, cleats still by the door.
**Mood:** Settled, in control, not anxious.

---

## Storyboard self-check (per skill)
- Relatable main character: yes — deliberately mainstream, not the extreme edge case, per the "built for all student-athletes" scope decision.
- Visceral problem: yes — concrete dollar amount, concrete deadline.
- Honest "oh crap" → "aha" gap: intentional — the crisis is the trigger, not something the product resolves in 5 days. Flagged and corrected during this session.
- Natural solution discovery: teammate word-of-mouth, not a magic find.
- Believable "aha": modest, process-driven, not a windfall.
- Aspirational but grounded "after" state: pipeline of small deals, not a single big payout.
